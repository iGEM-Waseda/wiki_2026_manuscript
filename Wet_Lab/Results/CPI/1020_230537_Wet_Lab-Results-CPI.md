# **CPI Experiment 1 – Reporter validation**

We evaluated whether the reporter plasmid J23100-loxP-TT-loxP-mRFP1 (L02), intended for future functional assessment of the CPI module, responds to Cre recombinase as designed. In the planned system, Pdp1_NTD-Cre delivered into target cells is expected to catalyze site-specific recombination between the two loxP sites on L02, excising the intervening terminator region. This design enables expression of the downstream red fluorescent protein mRFP1 only upon delivery of Pdp1_NTD-Cre into the target cells.

To verify that L02 functions as a Cre-responsive reporter, we treated the plasmid with purified Cre recombinase *in vitro* and compared the lengths of PCR amplicons obtained before and after treatment (see the [Experiments](https://2026.igem.wiki/waseda-tokyo/experiments/#cpi-experiments-1) section for the detailed protocol). The results are shown in Fig. 8.2.1.

:::c

PCR products were separated on a 2% agarose gel at 100 V for 20 min. In lane 2, a clear band was observed at approximately 989 bp, corresponding to the PCR amplicon expected before treatment with purified Cre recombinase. In contrast, lane 1 contained an additional band at approximately 785 bp, corresponding to the expected post-treatment amplicon. This additional band migrated to approximately the same position as the control band in lane 3. These results indicate that treatment with purified Cre recombinase excised the terminator region between the two loxP sites in J23100-loxP-TT-loxP-mRFP1 (L02).

Lane 1 also retained a band corresponding to the untreated amplicon, suggesting incomplete recombination, possibly due to the short incubation time. Although not tested in this study, extending the incubation time may increase the extent of recombination and reduce the residual 989 bp band, potentially yielding a single band at approximately 785 bp.

The control plasmid used for lane 3 was generated by PCR-mediated deletion of the sequence between the loxP sites that was expected to be excised by Cre recombinase. This PCR used primers already available to the Waseda-Tokyo team. However, these primers were designed to amplify a slightly shorter region than the expected post-Cre-treatment product. This difference likely explains why the band in lane 3 migrated slightly farther than the lower band in lane 1.

We also introduced Cre-treated L02, untreated L02, and J23100-loxP-mRFP1 (L03), in which the terminator region had been removed in advance, into BL21(DE3) cells and cultured them on agar plates. Colony coloration was visually inspected and compared, with the intensity of red coloration used as an indicator of mRFP1 expression (see the [Experiments](https://2026.igem.wiki/waseda-tokyo/experiments/#cpi-experiments-1) section for the detailed protocol). The results are shown in Fig. 8.2.2.

Typical creamy-white colonies were observed on the plate containing BL21(DE3) cells transformed with untreated L02 (Fig. 8.2.2 b). In contrast, some red colonies were observed on the plate containing cells transformed with Cre-treated L02 (Fig. 8.2.2 a). The coexistence of red and creamy-white colonies in Fig. 8.2.2 a likely reflects excision of the terminator region in only a subset of the L02 plasmids during treatment with purified Cre recombinase. This interpretation is consistent with the gel electrophoresis results shown in Fig. 8.2.1. Cells harboring L02 with the terminator region excised likely expressed mRFP1, resulting in red coloration, whereas cells harboring unrecombined L02 remained creamy-white.

# **CPI Experiment 2 – Pdp1_NTD-Cre activity validation**

Loading Cre into PVC particles requires fusion to the loading signal located in the N-terminal domain of Pdp1, a native PVC payload. However, it was unclear whether Pdp1_NTD-Cre, our intended payload, would retain recombinase activity in our experimental system. We therefore experimentally tested whether Pdp1_NTD-Cre could induce site-specific recombination between loxP sites inside cells.

We compared mRFP1 fluorescence under IPTG induction between cells harboring J23100-loxP-TT-loxP-mRFP1 (L02) together with either the Cre expression plasmid (C01) or the Pdp1_NTD-Cre expression plasmid (C02) (see the [Experiments](https://2026.igem.wiki/waseda-tokyo/experiments/#cpi-experiments-1) section for the detailed protocol). The results are shown in Fig. 8.2.3.

![mRFP1 fluorescence after Pdp1_NTD-Cre treatment](https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6144/wiki/experiments/cpi-experiment2-result-measurement-of-mrfp1-fluorescence-electrophoresis.avif =800x)

:::c

Fig. 8.2.3 Measurement of mRFP1 fluorescence after treatment with Pdp1_NTD-Cre

:::

:::c

(a) Measurement of mRFP1 fluorescence. IPTG was added to 5 mL cultures at a final concentration of 1 mM, followed by overnight incubation. Fluorescence was measured at excitation and emission wavelengths of 574 and 607 nm, respectively. (b) Bar chart showing the fluorescence intensity of each sample (n = 3). (c) PCR amplicons generated from plasmids extracted from *E. coli* cells under the indicated conditions. Lane 1: L02 alone; lane 2: L03 alone (control); lane 3: L02 + C01 with IPTG induction; lane 4: L02 + C02 with IPTG induction; lane 5: L02 + C01 without IPTG induction; lane 6: L02 + C02 without IPTG induction. The expected PCR amplicon lengths before and after Cre-mediated recombination are 989 and 785 bp, respectively ([detailed sequences](https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6144/wiki/experiments/cpi-experiment1-result-pcr-amplicon-length-before-and-after-treatment-with-cre.avif)).

:::

Cells harboring J23100-loxP-TT-loxP-mRFP1 (L02) together with either the Cre expression plasmid (C01) or the Pdp1_NTD-Cre expression plasmid (C02) exhibited nearly identical fluorescence intensities (Fig. 8.2.3 a,b). This result suggests that fusion of the Pdp1_NTD signal to the N-terminus of Cre does not substantially impair its recombinase activity under the conditions tested. In future experiments, Pdp1_NTD-Cre delivered by PVC into target bacteria harboring L02 is therefore expected to mediate site-specific recombination and induce mRFP1 expression.

The fluorescence intensities measured in cells harboring C01 + L02 or C02 + L02 were no more than 50% of that measured in cells harboring the positive-control plasmid J23100-loxP-mRFP1 (L03). One possible explanation is the difference in the number of plasmid types maintained by these cells. Cells harboring C01 + L02 or C02 + L02 carry two plasmid types, whereas cells harboring L03 carry only one. The latter may therefore have more cellular resources available for mRFP1 expression.

Plasmids were also extracted from each strain and subjected to PCR, followed by gel electrophoresis. Lane 1 showed a clear band at approximately 989 bp, corresponding to the amplicon expected before Cre-mediated recombination. In contrast, lanes 3–6 showed bands at approximately 785 bp, corresponding to the expected post-recombination amplicon and migrating similarly to the control band in lane 2. Whereas lane 1 in Fig. 8.2.1 (see CPI Experiment 1) contained both pre- and post-recombination bands, only the post-recombination band was detected in this experiment. This difference may reflect the longer period available for recombination: treatment with purified Cre was performed at 37°C for only 30 min, whereas cells expressing Cre or Pdp1_NTD-Cre were cultured overnight at 37°C with shaking.

Post-recombination amplicons were also detected in lanes 5 and 6, which corresponded to cells cultured without IPTG induction. This finding suggests that basal expression of Cre or Pdp1_NTD-Cre in the absence of IPTG was sufficient to induce recombination, indicating that lac repressor-mediated regulation did not completely suppress expression under these conditions.

# **CPI Experiment 3 – Pdp1_NTD-dependent payload loading validation**

We experimentally investigated whether Pdp1_NTD-Cre was loaded into PVC particles as intended. PVC preparations were purified and analyzed by SDS-PAGE to assess cargo incorporation. Successful loading was expected to yield a band corresponding to Pdp1_NTD-Cre in the purified fraction. Conversely, Pdp1_NTD-Cre that had not been incorporated was expected to be removed during purification and therefore remain undetectable by SDS-PAGE (see the [Experiments](https://2026.igem.wiki/waseda-tokyo/experiments/#cpi-experiments-1) section for the detailed protocol). The results are shown in Fig. 8.2.4.

:::

:::c

(a) Schematic overview of PVC purification by ultracentrifugation. PVC particles, which are large protein complexes, were purified through repeated conventional centrifugation and ultracentrifugation steps. V04: pAWP78-PVCpnf_PVC10_gp17_FLAG; D01: pBR322-Pdp1_NTD-Cre; D02: pBR322-ΔPdp1_NTD-Cre. Protein bands were visualized by Coomassie Brilliant Blue (CBB) staining. (c) Estimated protein concentrations of the purified PVC fractions, determined from absorbance at 280 nm (A280).

Because Pdp1_NTD-Cre has an estimated molecular mass of approximately 50 kDa, we examined bands near this size to assess its potential incorporation into PVC particles. However, unlike the wild-type PVC preparation (see [CPI Engineering](https://2026.igem.wiki/waseda-tokyo/engineering/) section), few bands were detectable from the second ultracentrifugation step onward, and no band corresponding to Pdp1_NTD-Cre could be confirmed (Fig. 8.2.4 b). No visible pellet was observed after the second ultracentrifugation step, suggesting that PVC particles may have been lost during purification. Consequently, we could not determine whether intact PVC particles had been successfully purified or whether they contained Pdp1_NTD-Cre. Nevertheless, although proteins were not detected by SDS-PAGE, A280 measurements indicated an estimated protein concentration of approximately 0.1 mg/mL in each purified PVC fraction (Fig. 8.2.4 c). We therefore decided to use these samples in an initial assay of attachment to the *E. coli* cell surface.

One possible explanation for the apparent loss is that modifications, including gp17 incorporation, disrupted PVC assembly or structural integrity, preventing the formation or maintenance of intact complexes. Whereas individual soluble proteins generally remain in the supernatant under the purification conditions, the large PVC complexes are recovered by ultracentrifugation as a pellet. Incomplete assembly or disruption into smaller structures could therefore cause PVC components to remain in the supernatant and escape recovery. However, lane 6 in Fig. 8.2.4 b, representing the supernatant after the second ultracentrifugation step, also contained few detectable bands. Thus, impaired assembly alone does not adequately explain the observed results.

# **CPI Experiment 4 – BL21(DE3) Cell Surface Binding Validation**

We experimentally examined whether PVC particles displaying gp17 could attach to the *E. coli* cell surface as designed. A FLAG tag had been incorporated into the PVC construct, enabling visualization by immunostaining with a green fluorescent label. Following incubation of *E. coli* with PVC, repeated centrifugation and washing were used to remove unbound particles. PVC particles attached to the cells were therefore expected to remain and produce green fluorescence colocalizing with the bacteria (see the [Experiments](https://2026.igem.wiki/waseda-tokyo/experiments/#cpi-experiments-1) section for the detailed protocol). The results are shown in Fig. 8.2.5.

![FV3000 observation](https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6144/wiki/experiments/cpi-experiment4-result-fv3000-observation-edit2.avif =1000x)

Blue: DAPI staining of *E. coli* nucleoids. Green: Alexa Fluor 488 immunolabeling of FLAG-tagged PVC particles.

DAPI staining clearly delineated the *E. coli* nucleoids, allowing the cells to be localized. Very weak green fluorescence was observed in all three L02 samples. Its similar appearance in PVC-treated and untreated samples suggested that the signal was not attributable to PVC. In contrast, L03 cells exhibited strong fluorescence in the green channel. However, as with L02, no marked difference was observed between PVC-treated and untreated samples, providing no evidence of a PVC-specific signal. Because L03 constitutively expresses mRFP1, this fluorescence may have resulted from spectral bleed-through into the green detection channel.

The weak green fluorescence observed in L02 cells could reflect bacterial autofluorescence or a contribution from leaky mRFP1 expression. Imaging plasmid-free BL21(DE3) cells under the same conditions would be required to help distinguish these possibilities.

# **CPI Experiment 5 – Payload Injection into BL21(DE3) Validation**

We experimentally assessed whether PVC could deliver its payload into the *E. coli* cytoplasm following attachment to the cell surface. After PVC addition, the cells were cultured overnight, and OD₆₀₀ and mRFP1 fluorescence were measured the following day. Using the reporter system validated in CPI Experiments 1 and 2, successful intracellular delivery of Pdp1_NTD-Cre was expected to induce mRFP1 expression and produce red fluorescence (see the [Experiments](https://2026.igem.wiki/waseda-tokyo/experiments/#cpi-experiments-1) section for the detailed protocol). The results are shown in Fig. 8.2.6.

![OD₆₀₀ and mRFP1 Fluorescence Intensity Following PVC Treatment](https://static.igem.wiki/teams/6144/wiki/experiments/cpi-experiment5-result-od600-and-mrfp1-fluorescence-edit.avif =500x)

:::c

Fig. 8.2.6 OD₆₀₀ and mRFP1 Fluorescence Intensity Following PVC Treatment

:::

:::c

(a) Measured OD₆₀₀ values. (b) Measured mRFP1 fluorescence intensity. (c) mRFP1 fluorescence intensity normalized to OD₆₀₀. Following incubation with PVC for 3 h, the entire reaction mixture was transferred to LB medium and cultured overnight before measurement. L02: J23100-loxP-TT-loxP-mRFP1; L03: J23100-loxP-mRFP1 (positive control).

Endpoint OD₆₀₀ values were broadly comparable across PVC treatment conditions for both L02 and L03 (Fig. 8.2.6 a). In contrast, red fluorescence differed markedly between the two strains: L02 consistently exhibited low fluorescence regardless of PVC addition, whereas L03 maintained high fluorescence under all conditions (Fig. 8.2.6 b). Thus, no detectable induction of mRFP1 expression was observed in PVC-treated L02 cells, suggesting that functional delivery of Pdp1_NTD-Cre had not occurred under the conditions tested.

Interpreting this outcome requires considering the successive steps involved in PVC-mediated payload delivery. One possibility is that the injection machinery failed to function correctly: the needle may not have penetrated sufficiently into the bacterial cell, or its deployment may not have occurred. Injection also requires prior attachment to the bacterial surface. Because attachment was not conclusively demonstrated, failure at this step could likewise account for the absence of reporter activation. An additional uncertainty concerns cargo loading during PVC assembly. Even if attachment and injection functioned as intended, the absence of Pdp1_NTD-Cre within the particles would prevent delivery of the reporter-activating cargo and, consequently, detectable mRFP1 fluorescence. Uncertainty also remains as to whether sufficient PVC particles were recovered during purification. The absence of the expected bands in Fig. 8.2.4 b prevented confirmation of successful PVC purification. Moreover, the low protein concentrations shown in Fig. 8.2.4 c suggest that, even if PVC particles were present, their abundance may have been insufficient for the injection assay.

These findings suggest that future work should first focus on improving PVC recovery while preserving particle integrity. One approach would be to optimize the ultracentrifugation-based purification procedure, as variations in *E. coli* culture duration, centrifugation speed, and buffer volume may affect recovery. The direct use of PVC-containing *E. coli* lysates in attachment and injection assays should also be considered as an alternative to purification. Following detergent removal, for example by ultrafiltration, the recovered preparation could be added to BL21(DE3) cultures to assess whether PVC particles retained in the lysate remain functional. Because SDS-PAGE did not allow a clear assessment of Pdp1_NTD-Cre loading, Western blotting with an anti-Cre antibody would provide a more sensitive means of detecting the cargo in PVC-containing fractions. Systematically revisiting each preceding step should help identify where the current design or experimental workflow is limiting delivery.
